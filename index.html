Selection & interpretation

These are selected qualitative examples from the same 473 filtered SeG motions used in the paper. The selection emphasizes large Semantic Score gains over the better baseline and cases where both baselines fail the numerical criteria while FAMoR passes. Distinct gesture families are included to show different motion patterns. Page-turning and flute-playing gestures connect the comparison to the paper’s examples.

The clips are illustrative and are not a random sample or a user study. Their selection does not estimate human preference or the frequency of an improvement across the dataset. Per-motion measurements are shown for all three methods, including values that do not favor FAMoR.

We compare FAMoR with baselines adapted from GMR [1] and vision-language-guided retargeting [2]. Both baselines are adapted with the shared modules. Fixed Pipeline uses the IK-only output, consistent with the paper’s method comparison.

All methods use the same source timestamps, sampled at 15 frames per second without pose interpolation. Each camera remains fixed within a clip and is fitted to that clip’s full motion. The front/side control switches all robot views together. The human source remains in front view. Wrist markers are orange on the right and cyan on the left.

Semantic Score ↑
Preservation of source anchor–effector spatial relations. Higher is better.
Amplitude ratio
Robot movement extent relative to the source. A value near 1 indicates similar extent. Larger is not always better.
Collision-free / velocity compliance
Saved self-collision and nonfinger joint-velocity checks. Success requires both to pass over the complete motion. A short video alone cannot establish these checks.
